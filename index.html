Europe Work Visa Guide 2026

Which European countries give work visas to Bangladeshis, what each one asks for, where you submit the visa, and where to find a job offer first.

Job offer first

Almost every country needs an employer to apply for your permit before you apply for a visa.

Easiest without a degree

Romania and Croatia, then Italy, Greece and Malta.

Best for developers

Germany's IT route, the Netherlands, Sweden, Ireland, and Spain's remote-work visa.

Avoid for now

Hungary (closed), Serbia and North Macedonia (BMET clearance on hold), Russia and Belarus.

Your plan

A plan built for your profile

EDU SSC (no HSC) EDU GED is optional SKILL Web & app developer LANG Good English ALSO Farm and supervisor work

Your developer experience matters more than your certificates. Several countries give IT workers a visa without a degree if you can prove about three years of work and the job pays enough. Run Plan A and Plan B together, and keep Plan C ready as a backup.

Do this first

Collect proof of your experience. Germany's IT route and Spain's remote-work visa both accept about 3 years of documented work instead of a degree. Get experience letters on company letterhead (dates, job title, tech stack), contracts, payslips or bank statements, tax returns, freelance invoices and platform history, plus links to your live apps and GitHub.

A

Tech job with visa sponsorship

Best fit
Why it fits you
No degree is needed for Germany's IT route, the Netherlands, Sweden or Ireland, as long as the salary and your experience are right.
Targets
  • Germany: EU Blue Card for IT specialists, €45,934 a year, 3 years' experience in the last 7.
  • Netherlands: €4,357 a month under 30 (€5,942 at 30+), recognised-sponsor employer.
  • Sweden: SEK 34,470 a month (about €3,050), no education rule.
  • Ireland: €36,605 a year, experience can replace a degree, English-speaking.
  • Poland: big IT sector; the employer gets your work permit.
Steps
  1. Collect proof of 3+ years' experience (see above).
  2. Take IELTS and aim for 6.5 or higher to prove your English.
  3. Polish your GitHub and 2–3 live projects; write a one-page English CV.
  4. Apply to 10+ jobs a week on the sponsorship boards; contact companies on sponsor lists.
  5. After an offer, the employer handles the permit. Germany, the Netherlands and Sweden take your visa in Dhaka.

B

Remote job, then a digital-nomad visa

Run in parallel
Why it fits you
You can work for foreign companies or clients from Dhaka now, then move. Spain accepts 3 years' experience instead of a degree.
Targets
  • Spain: €2,849 a month of remote income; the residence permit can be renewed.
  • Portugal: €3,680 a month plus €11,040 in savings.
  • Greece: €3,500 a month net. Malta: €42,000 a year.
  • Croatia: €3,622.50 a month, but only up to 18 months.
Steps
  1. Get a steady remote job or long-term clients outside the country you want to move to.
  2. Take your pay through your bank so every month is on record; keep contracts.
  3. Build at least 3–6 months of steady income before you apply (Portugal checks the last 3 months).
  4. Apply for Spain at the Spanish Embassy in Dhaka (BLS); Portugal through its embassy in New Delhi.

C

Farm or supervisor work

Backup
Why it fits you
Your SSC is enough and no degree is needed. Pay is low and the permit ties you to one employer, so treat it as a backup.
Targets
  • Greece: BOESL's government seasonal farm programme (4,000 a year).
  • Italy: seasonal farm quota; the farm files for you on the 12 January 2027 click day.
  • Romania: farm and construction jobs on the 289-job shortage list.
  • Croatia: seasonal tourism and farm permits for up to 3 years.
Steps
  1. Register on BMET's Overseas Employment Platform and follow BOESL circulars.
  2. Use only licensed agencies; check the RL number before paying anything.
  3. For supervisor jobs, get letters proving you supervised people.
  4. For Italy, the employer must prepare your file before the January 2027 click day.

Do you need the GED?

Not for these visas. Work visas for developers look at your experience and the salary, not school certificates. A GED also does not open Germany's Opportunity Card or German public universities. Take it only if an employer asks for high-school equivalency, or if you later want to study abroad.

        Section A

        Non-skilled and semi-skilled workers

        Factory, construction, farm, warehouse, cleaning, kitchen and hotel work. No degree needed; SSC or HSC is fine. Difficulty 1 is easiest.

        Country notes

        Where non-skilled workers find jobs

        Most real offers come through licensed agencies and government programmes, not job websites.

          Section B

          Skilled workers

          Degree, polytechnic diploma, recognised trade certificate, IT, nursing or engineering. Tradespeople with certificates and experience can also use the shortage-job routes in Germany, Austria, Spain, France, Denmark and Norway.
